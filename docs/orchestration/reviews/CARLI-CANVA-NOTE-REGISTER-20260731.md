## How completion works

Every source item has two separate gates:

1. `Capture` is checked when the Canva direction has been durably transcribed here.
2. `Delivery` is checked only after the exact affected occurrences have been changed or intentionally excluded, rebuilt into clean and annotated review surfaces, and independently verified.

No source item may be called complete from capture alone.

## Disposition codes

- `ready-copy-review`: exact supplied wording or a clear keep/remove instruction is ready for Nate's review.
- `ready-design-review`: the visual or structural direction is clear but still needs a rendered choice or QA.
- `needs-fact-check`: public factual, ingredient, health, process, storage, origin or policy wording needs authority before use.
- `needs-catalogue`: product facts, images, variants or selling information wait for catalogue reconciliation.
- `needs-asset`: an approved image or other client asset is required.
- `needs-consent`: testimonial use remains blocked by permission and attribution requirements.
- `needs-Nate`: Carli raised a question or offered a direction that still needs Nate's decision.
- `keep`: Carli explicitly marked the existing item to remain.

3. Pseudonyms do not remove the testimonial consent requirement. Testimonials remain excluded from the clean surface.
4. Product names, images, weights, prices, availability, ingredients and selling options remain blocked on the fresh WooCommerce export and approved retail catalogue.
5. Clean review must never expose unresolved, disabled-looking, placeholder or obviously unfinished content.
6. Each delivery checkbox requires occurrence evidence, clean/annotated parity where applicable, responsive QA and an independent check.
